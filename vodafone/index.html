The challenge — make a complex setup feel connected.

A traveller needs more than an eSIM purchase page. They need to choose a suitable plan, verify their identity, pay, install the connection and find help when a step needs attention.

I worked on Vodafone Fiji’s desktop service and mobile verification experience. The scope extended from account access and group purchases to usage, billing and support.

How might we keep travellers oriented across these steps, especially when they switch devices or need to try again?

Research — uncertainty is part of the task.

A retrospective review of public traveller discussions and provider guidance highlighted three recurring questions to examine in the designs.

Will this connection work for me?

Device eligibility and plan terms need to be understandable before someone commits to a purchase.

Why is this information needed?

Passport and selfie capture introduce questions about trust, image quality and how to correct a failed check.

Am I ready to use it?

Payment, installation and activation need distinct explanations so a completed transaction does not imply that every setup step is finished.

This review followed the 2025 design work. Public examples establish useful questions, not their frequency among Vodafone Fiji customers. Read the evidence review ↗

Decision one — recommend a plan around the trip.

The problem: travellers need a plan that fits their stay without comparing every package.

The design response: after entering their travel dates, they select AI-Powered Recommendation to receive a plan suited to the trip’s duration.

They can review and choose the suggestion, or browse Data Only and Combo Plans.

Decision two — guide verification without losing progress.

The problem: verification combines a sensitive document, camera capture, extracted details and a result. An unclear handoff or failed check can leave someone unsure how to continue.

The design response: break the task into capture guidance, detail review and recovery. Desktop offers QR continuation on mobile alongside a “Stay on PC” route. People can inspect extracted information and reach re-upload or manual-help paths.

The trade-off: switching devices introduces another point of uncertainty. The next iteration should explain what progress carries over, when the session expires and how to resume after an interruption.

Decision three — separate payment from readiness.

The problem: payment success answers a transaction question. It does not tell the traveller whether installation is complete or when the connection becomes active.

The design response: provide confirmation, installation entry points and a recovery route for unsuccessful payment. Retry and support stay close to the failed step.

The next refinement: use separate purchase, installation and activation labels, with the next action beside each. Activation wording must reflect the customer’s actual booking and carrier policy.

Decision four — keep help close to the connection.

The problem: the service continues after checkout. Travellers need to identify the right eSIM, understand its usage and expiry, and resolve issues during their trip.

The design response: connect the dashboard, named eSIMs, plan management, billing and notifications. FAQs, chat, calls and ticket statuses support both immediate questions and ongoing requests.

The next refinement: preserve the selected connection and relevant issue when entering support. The person should understand what information is shared and how to return to their task.

Across devices — keep the task familiar.

Desktop gives plan comparison and account management more space. Mobile verification prioritises the camera task, short instructions and readable personal information in a single column.

Vodafone red, light working surfaces and recurring form and action patterns provide continuity. The goal is to preserve the meaning of each action while adapting its layout to the device.

The next responsive checks are camera permissions, keyboard visibility, text enlargement and recovery from field errors.

Outcome — a connected service design.

The design scope covers acquisition, identity verification, purchase, management and support. Its strongest contribution is making room for review, device handoff and recovery alongside the successful path.

Usability and business impact still need validation. The next evaluation should follow complete tasks rather than assess isolated screens.

Reflection — make the next step clear.

The most useful next iteration is tighter continuity: consistent plan information, explicit setup states and recovery that returns people to their task.

A simple-looking screen is only part of the work. The experience needs to make sense when a person moves between screens, devices and moments of uncertainty.
